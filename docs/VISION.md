# MediMol vision

**Today:** for one hard-to-treat cancer, MediMol ranks drugs that are already approved and affordable by two kinds of evidence: physics (docking) and lab data (ChEMBL). It shows how far to trust each signal (stability, enrichment, applicability domain) and hands a lab a shortlist it can actually afford to test.

**Why it matters:** repurposed generics skip most of the cost and time of new-drug development, and they are the drugs that low-resource health systems can buy.

**Next steps, each one honest and small:**
1. **More cancers.** The bundle format is cancer-agnostic: a new target list and a new curation sheet give a new run.
2. **Close the loop.** Wet-lab results logged by the Bench Companion flow back into the bundle as measured labels, and the next model learns from them.
3. **Open shortlists.** Publish each versioned bundle so any student lab can reproduce and extend it.
4. **Access data at global scale.** Add national essential-medicines lists beyond WHO's to show where a drug is obtainable, country by country.

**What it will never do:** treat patients, give medical advice, or claim that a drug works without lab and clinical evidence.
